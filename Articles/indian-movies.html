Stop Skipping the Song-and-Dance Numbers: Indian Films for Everyone

Six films to start with, ranked by how quickly they will make you feel like an idiot for waiting this long

"The whole world is a family." — Sanskrit proverb, also basically the thesis of every Indian film ever made

The usual excuse is the runtime. Three hours is too long. The songs are too many. The emotions are too big. All of that is true, and none of it is a reason not to watch. Indian cinema does not underplay things, and that is not a flaw in the filmmaking. It is a different set of priorities, one that produces films capable of making an entire theater cry and cheer inside the same ten minutes. Once you accept that, the runtime stops being an obstacle.

This list is for people who have either never watched an Indian film or watched one once and bounced off it. These are not the most critically prestigious picks or the most beloved by hardcore fans. They are the ones that tend to work on people who did not grow up with this cinema, ranked by how reliably they land.

1

Dil Chahta Hai (2001)

Farhan Akhtar · Aamir Khan, Saif Ali Khan, Akshaye Khanna · 183 min
★★★★★

Three friends graduate college and immediately start figuring out that the version of their lives they planned for does not match the one they are actually living. Farhan Akhtar's directorial debut is one of the best films about male friendship ever made, full stop, from any country. It does not sentimentalize its characters or let them off the hook for being difficult, and the chemistry between the three leads is so natural that the film never feels scripted. There are songs. They are good. Start here.

2

Dangal (2016)

Nitesh Tiwari · Aamir Khan, Fatima Sana Shaikh · 161 min
★★★★★

A father in a village in Haryana trains his daughters to become wrestlers against the wishes of everyone around him, including, for a long stretch, the daughters themselves. The film is complicated about whether his methods are admirable or overbearing and does not pretend those two things are easy to separate. What makes it extraordinary is that the sports sequences are genuinely great, the performances are genuinely great, and the film earns its finale completely. This one grossed over 300 million dollars in China alone. The audience knew what they were watching.

3

3 Idiots (2009)

Rajkumar Hirani · Aamir Khan, R. Madhavan, Sharman Joshi · 170 min
★★★★½

Three engineering students survive one of India's most competitive universities while one of them quietly refuses to stop asking why any of it matters. The film is a comedy that somehow also has something real to say about education, ambition, and the distance between what parents want and what children are. It is funny in a way that does not require cultural context to land, which makes it one of the most reliable first Indian films for anyone who needs a comfortable entry point. Aamir Khan appears in three of the top four spots on this list because he spent twenty years making almost no bad choices.

4

Lagaan (2001)

Ashutosh Gowariker · Aamir Khan, Gracy Singh · 224 min
★★★★½

A village in colonial India challenges its British rulers to a cricket match to avoid paying three years of taxes. That premise sounds like it should not work, and Lagaan makes it work for nearly four hours. The match itself is one of the most sustained pieces of sports filmmaking in any language, built up over two-plus hours of character work so that every run and every wicket carries genuine stakes. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and lost, which remains one of the more embarrassing decisions in that category's history.

5

Baahubali: The Beginning (2015)

S.S. Rajamouli · Prabhas, Rana Daggubati · 159 min
★★★★

Pure mythological spectacle, the kind that Indian cinema had never attempted at this scale and largely pulled off. The story is a fantasy epic with a dual hero structure, a clear villain you will immediately hate, and a waterfall sequence in the opening ten minutes that announces exactly what kind of film you are watching. It is uneven in places and ends on a cliffhanger that requires watching the sequel, but as a demonstration of what Telugu cinema is capable of when it commits fully, there is nothing else like it. Watch it loud.

6

PK (2014)

Rajkumar Hirani · Aamir Khan, Anushka Sharma · 153 min
★★★★

An alien stranded on Earth tries to find his way home and keeps getting derailed by the fact that nobody can agree on what God wants. The film is a comedy that is genuinely critical of religious exploitation in a way that made it one of the most controversial Indian releases in years and also one of the highest-grossing. It is lighter than most of the films on this list and moves faster, which makes it a good pick if the longer runtimes feel daunting. The performance from Aamir Khan is physical and committed in a way that carries the whole thing.

Bollywood is not the whole of Indian cinema, and this list barely scratches the surface of what Telugu, Tamil, and Malayalam filmmakers have been doing for decades. But these six are where most people start, and most people who start here do not stop at six.
