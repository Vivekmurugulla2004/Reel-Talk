The Amazing Spider-Man 2

A Hundred Tom Holland Movies Over This Again

Superhero/Action ‧ 142 minutes ‧ PG-13 ‧ 2014 ‧ 5 min read

Rating ★☆☆☆☆ 1 / 5

"I’ve been invisible my whole life."

The Amazing Spider-Man 2 has undergone one of the strangest reputation shifts in the genre. Directed by Marc Webb and starring Andrew Garfield, Emma Stone, Jamie Foxx, and Dane DeHaan, the 2014 film is now suddenly hailed in some corners as a misunderstood masterpiece, with people praising Garfield's performance, applauding its CGI, and even defending its handling of the villains. Some go as far as to argue that it was unfairly treated and deserved a third installment.

Let's be absolutely clear: TASM 2 is not a good movie. It isn't secretly brilliant, it isn't misunderstood, and it certainly isn't some hidden gem buried beneath studio interference. It is one of the most creatively bankrupt films ever made, a movie so obsessed with building a cinematic universe that it forgets to tell a coherent story. Nearly every major narrative decision exists to satisfy Sony's corporate roadmap rather than the needs of the characters or the plot.

The script is a mess from beginning to end. Harry Osborn suddenly appears as Peter's supposedly lifelong best friend despite never being mentioned once in the previous film. There is no emotional foundation, no believable history, and no reason to care when their relationship falls apart. Electro begins with the potential to be an interesting tragic figure but is reduced to an awkward caricature whose motivations shift depending on what the plot needs.

The pacing is equally disastrous. Instead of focusing on one compelling narrative, the movie juggles Peter and Gwen's relationship, Aunt May's hidden secrets, Oscorp conspiracies, Electro's origin, Harry's disease, the Sinister Six setup, Peter's parents, Rhino, and enough sequel bait to fill an entire post-credits film. None of these storylines receive the development they deserve because the film refuses to prioritize.

Then there's the infamous Richard Parker subway video, perhaps one of the single worst additions to Spider-Man mythology ever put on screen. Spider-Man has always represented the idea that anyone, regardless of background or status, could become a hero if they chose responsibility over selfishness. This film throws that philosophy in the trash, explaining that only Peter could have survived the spider bite because of his unique genetic heritage. He isn't Spider-Man because an ordinary kid rose to extraordinary circumstances. He's Spider-Man because he comes from a special bloodline.

Even Gwen Stacy's death, arguably the film's strongest scene, cannot save it. Yes, it is emotionally effective. Yes, Garfield delivers one of the best performances of his career. But one powerful scene does not erase two hours of bloated storytelling, confused characterization, and relentless franchise setup. A great ending cannot magically fix everything that came before it.

Beneath all the corporate mandates is the outline of a genuinely good Spider-Man movie, but that movie never made it to the screen. The Amazing Spider-Man 2 isn't underrated, and it isn't even mediocre. It's a bloated, unfocused, studio-driven failure that fundamentally misunderstands Spider-Man while sabotaging its own franchise. The Amazing Spider-Man 2 gets a 1 out of 5.

(Sorry, little Timmy - only Mr. Parker gets the spider powers.)
