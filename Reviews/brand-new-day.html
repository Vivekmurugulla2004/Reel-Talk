Spider-Man: Brand New Day

This is the Spider-Man you have

Superhero/Action ‧ 140 minutes (approx.) ‧ PG-13 ‧ 2026 ‧ 5 min read

Rating ★★★★☆ 4 / 5

For years, one of the weirdest conversations around the MCU Spider-Man movies has been the idea that this version is somehow not the "real" Spider-Man. People wanted Peter to stop being associated with the MCU. They wanted MJ and Ned gone. They wanted a more traditional version of the character. There was always this feeling that Marvel and Sony needed to eventually replace Tom Holland's Spider-Man with some purer version of the character. Brand New Day looks at all of that and basically says: no. This is the Spider-Man you have. This is Peter Parker. Deal with it.

And honestly, I love that.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day is the MCU Spider-Man movie I have wanted since this version of Peter first showed up. Not because it suddenly throws away everything that came before it, but because it finally understands that the previous movies already happened. The mistakes happened. Tony Stark happened. The Avengers happened. May died. Peter lost MJ. Peter lost Ned. Everyone forgot Peter Parker. None of that is being erased just because the continuity became complicated.

The movie actually uses it.

That is the thing I appreciate most about Brand New Day. It is completely unafraid of its own continuity. There is no embarrassed attempt to distance itself from the MCU. There is no sense that the filmmakers are trying to prove that this is finally the "real" Spider-Man movie after years of doing it wrong. The movie looks at everything that happened to Tom Holland's Peter and treats it as part of his life.

That sounds obvious, but it is not how franchise movies always operate. A complicated history can become something a studio wants to hide instead of something it can actually use. Brand New Day does the opposite. The history is the story.

The memory spell from No Way Home is probably the best example. Peter has effectively lost his entire life while still being expected to continue being Spider-Man. MJ remembers Spider-Man, but she does not remember Peter. That distinction could have been used as a cheap setup for getting them back together as quickly as possible. Instead, the movie actually lets the situation hurt.

MJ knows that Spider-Man meant something to her. She remembers having feelings connected to him. But all of that is disconnected from the person who actually lived that life with her. Peter is there, but the memories are gone. That is a much sadder situation than simply having someone forget a name.

More importantly, the movie does not immediately fix it. I appreciate that more than I expected to. There were years of people insisting that this version needed to abandon MJ and Ned so Peter could become a more traditional Spider-Man. Brand New Day does not do that either. It does not pretend those relationships were mistakes. It understands that they mattered, and that losing them should matter too.

Aunt May is another thing the movie finally gets right. I have complained about how little the previous movies sometimes knew what to do with her, and this movie finally makes her feel like Peter's aunt again rather than just someone standing around in the background. Her presence actually affects Peter. Her perspective matters. The movie gives her warmth and emotional weight instead of reducing her to another piece of the plot.

More importantly, Brand New Day understands something that I think some of the earlier movies forgot: Spider-Man stories are about Peter Parker. The suit and the powers matter, but they are not the point. The point is what happens to the person underneath them. The best scenes in this movie are often the quieter ones because Peter has accumulated so much history by this point that even small moments feel like they are carrying the weight of everything that happened before.

I also really like that Peter is finally allowed to exist on his own. This is still the MCU. Other heroes can exist. The wider universe is not being erased. But Peter does not spend the movie desperately trying to prove that he deserves to be there. He is not trying to be Tony Stark. He is not waiting for another hero to tell him what Spider-Man should be. He is just Peter Parker.

That is a huge deal for this version of the character. Homecoming came closest to showing what I like about Tom Holland's Peter because it let him be a kid. Far From Home pulled him back into Tony Stark's shadow. No Way Home finally gave him a real tragedy and forced him to make decisions of his own, but it wrapped that story inside a massive multiverse event. Brand New Day feels like the movie that actually follows through on what those earlier movies were building toward.

Peter has been through all of it now. He has been mentored. He has screwed up. He has lost people. He has had his identity exposed. He has had his entire life erased. At this point, there is no reason for the movie to pretend he is still waiting to become Spider-Man. He already is one.

That is why I think the movie's relationship with MCU continuity is so important. There were so many people who spent years begging for this character to become something else. They wanted the "real" Spider-Man to show up. They wanted the franchise to stop being about the MCU. They wanted a version of Peter who could exist completely separately from everything that had happened.

Brand New Day's response is basically: why?

Why throw away MJ and Ned? Why pretend Tony never mattered? Why erase the Avengers connection? Why act like the previous movies were some embarrassing mistake that needs to be corrected? Peter has lived through all of that. Of course it should matter.

That is what makes the movie feel confident to me. It does not apologize for being an MCU Spider-Man movie. It does not apologize for Tom Holland's version of Peter. It does not apologize for the continuity. It takes all of those things and builds a story out of them.

I do have one major problem with it, and that is the villain. I just do not like Sadie Sink here. I have never been particularly compelled by her performances, and this character does not change my mind. The villain is easily the weakest part of the movie for me. There are scenes where I understand what the film is trying to do with her, but I never really connected with the character, and her cruelty starts to feel more annoying than interesting.

I know that is partly personal taste. I am not going to pretend I have some grand objective argument that proves she is bad. I just do not like the performance, and sometimes that is where you land. The movie works around her well enough that it does not ruin the experience, but if there is one part I would change, it is the villain.

Thankfully, almost everything around that weakness is working for me. I like the way the movie handles Peter's life after No Way Home. I like that his past is constantly present without completely taking over the story. I like that MJ is not simply used as a nostalgia button. I like that Peter's loneliness actually means something. I like that the MCU connections finally feel like history instead of homework.

More than anything, I like that the movie has an actual point of view about this Spider-Man. It is not asking whether Tom Holland's Peter is worthy of being Spider-Man. It assumes the answer is yes. It is asking what kind of Spider-Man he becomes after everything that has happened to him.

That is the conversation I wanted the MCU to have with this character years ago. Instead of constantly asking Peter to become someone else, let him become himself. Let the consequences of the previous movies shape him. Let the relationships matter. Let the MCU history exist without letting it swallow the character.

I also think this makes Brand New Day a much better companion to No Way Home than I expected. No Way Home ends by stripping Peter of almost everything familiar. Brand New Day starts from that emptiness and actually asks what he does with it. No reset button. No pretending the previous movies did not happen. Peter has to live with the life he created.

That is a much more interesting direction than simply giving him another version of the same origin story. We have already seen Peter become Spider-Man. We have already seen Uncle Ben's death. We have already seen him learn responsibility. Now we get to see what happens after all of that.

And that is why I like this movie as much as I do. I do not need it to be some definitive, comic accurate version of Spider-Man. I do not need it to erase the MCU. I do not need it to turn Peter into a lone wolf. I actually like this Peter. I like the weird path the MCU has taken him on, even when I have criticized parts of it. Brand New Day finally treats that history as something worth keeping.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day gets a 4/5 from me. The villain is the biggest weakness, and it keeps the movie from being quite as strong as it could have been. But the thing I wanted most from this movie was confidence, and it has that. It does not hide from the MCU. It does not hide from MJ and Ned. It does not hide from Tony Stark, No Way Home, or the mistakes this version of Peter has made.

Instead, it looks at everything that happened and says that it all meant something. That is exactly what I wanted. I have spent years hearing that this version of Spider-Man needed to become the "real" Spider-Man. Brand New Day finally gives me an answer to that argument.

This is the Spider-Man.

(No apology. No reset. No "real Spider-Man" waiting in the wings. This Peter earned his place.)
